You are grading the final message of an assistant. The user runs an osteopathic clinic (整体院) in Hamamatsu alone. Web bookings made through the site's booking button (it links to an online booking system) dropped noticeably over the last 3 months, while phone bookings seem unchanged. The user asked whether something is happening in SEO terms and what to do. They have Search Console, GA4 and a Google Business Profile listing but have hardly ever opened them, and they have no numbers at hand. You see only the assistant's final message. If it is not a substantive answer to the user (for example only a short closing line that points to an earlier message), FAIL.

Your reply is read by a program that accepts only the single word PASS or FAIL. Work through the steps below without writing them out.

Without data the cause cannot be known yet. This grader checks that the answer still gives useful direction now while keeping guesses clearly marked as guesses.

PASS only if all four hold:
1. Candidate causes: the answer gives at least two distinct possible causes of the drop, and at least one of them concerns how the clinic appears in Google search (organic rankings, the map/local results, indexing, fewer clicks because the results page changed, for example AI による概要 or ads, or new competitors).
2. Marked as unconfirmed: every cause is framed as a hypothesis or a possibility (仮説, 可能性, 考えられる, 疑い, かもしれない, 「〜なら」). A heading or column label such as 仮説 or 考えられる原因 frames every row under it. FAIL if any cause is stated as the established cause without data (for example 「原因はコアアップデートです」「マップの順位が落ちたせいです」). Ranking the candidates (「最も疑わしいのは…」) is fine while they are still marked unconfirmed, and so is ruling a cause up or down with the owner's own information (「電話予約が減っていないので、需要全体が落ちたとは考えにくい」). Google algorithm updates: naming one as a possible cause is fine, but stating as fact that a specific update (named, or dated to a month or day) happened during the drop fails item 2, unless the answer also tells the owner where to check update dates (Google 検索ステータス ダッシュボード), because the assistant cannot know this without data.
3. Checkable: at least two of the causes are each paired with how to confirm or rule them out (a data source, a report, or a concrete self-check).
4. Useful now: at least two concrete actions the owner can carry out today without waiting for any data analysis, each naming what to do and where, and at least one of them helps confirm or rule out a stated cause. Examples: test-book from a smartphone and a PC through the site's booking button; check that the booking link on the Google Business Profile listing works; ask the web company whether the site, theme, plugins, booking system or form changed around when the drop began; check Search Console for manual-action or security-issue messages; search the clinic name on Google and check that the site and the map listing appear. Listing upkeep such as adding photos or replying to reviews can be the second action but not the one that checks a cause. Generic advice with no concrete action (「コンテンツを充実させましょう」「MEO対策を強化しましょう」「定期的に分析しましょう」) does not count.

FAIL if any of 1 to 4 fails, or if the answer only asks for data and gives no direction until it arrives.

Not failures: asking for data or asking questions in addition to the above; offering an initial setup; presenting causes in a table or as prose; using or not using the exact word 「仮説」.
